LOGISTICS

Are your freight invoices correct?

Freight invoices contain valuable information about transport costs, routes and services. Comparing them with agreed prices can reveal unusual charges that deserve a closer look.

THE PROBLEM

Why are freight invoices difficult to verify?

High invoice volumes, incomplete comparisons and manual checks can make billing discrepancies difficult to identify.

  1. Many invoices

    Logistics teams receive numerous carrier invoices. Each one may require checking the price, service and applicable surcharges.

  2. Manual judgement

    Employees often rely on experience and plausibility when deciding whether an invoice looks correct. Unusual details can be overlooked.

  3. Automated billing

    Carrier billing systems process charges automatically. Incorrect rates or surcharges may remain unnoticed unless invoices are checked against the agreed terms.

  4. Hidden discrepancies

    Small differences between agreed and billed prices can accumulate across many shipments if invoices are not checked systematically.

DATA PREPARATION

Prepare the data before building a model.

Useful analysis starts with relevant historical records. The information must be organised and checked before a model can learn from it.

  1. Collect the data

    Gather historical invoices from previous months or years, together with available contracts, rate agreements and shipment records.

  2. Standardise the data

    Organise invoice number, provider, service, date, amount, route and other relevant fields into a consistent format.

  3. Label verified cases

    Review historical invoices against the applicable contract, shipment details and service records. Label cases only when there is sufficient evidence that they are correct or incorrect.

TECHNICAL SOLUTION

Find patterns that help identify unusual invoices.

Data science methods can help distinguish ordinary cost patterns from cases that need closer investigation.

  1. Identify useful predictors

    Explore which variables, such as transport mode, route, service type and cost components, help distinguish verified invoices from known errors.

  2. Train and test the model

    Use separate training and test data. Evaluate how well the model identifies known cases and repeat the process when the results are not reliable enough.

  3. Validate the results

    Test whether the useful patterns remain reliable on previously unseen data. Noisy data and misleading relationships must be considered before using the model.

PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION

From new invoices to a focused review.

Once validated, the model can support recurring invoice checks and help employees focus on the cases that need attention.

  1. Feed new data

    Provide new daily or weekly invoices in the same standardised format used to develop the model.

  2. Process the invoices

    The model evaluates the available information and compares each invoice with patterns learned from historical data.

  3. Review flagged cases

    Invoices can be marked as potentially unusual or within the expected range. A flagged invoice needs a second check; it is not automatically an error.
